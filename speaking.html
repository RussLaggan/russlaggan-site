The Jellyfish Method

Tentacles out. People swim into the work.

A jellyfish has no hard skeleton. It puts tentacles in the water and contact comes to it. That is the whole picture of the talk. You become the thing people run into, because you were already useful where they were. Chasing them makes them run.

The talk is for an organization that wants scale without turning into a brittle org chart. Real estate is where Russ built it. The shape is not limited to real estate.

Put the value where they already are

The tentacles are useful work, sent to people who already have a reason to know you. Partners, past customers, the operators beside the transaction. They forward it because it makes them look competent. You become the source without making the conversation about yourself.

A gallon is a gallon

There is no such thing as 110 percent. If the new effort only grows by stealing hours from the work that already pays the bills, the core dips, you panic, and the new effort dies. The Coffee Mug Test is that point, live, with a vessel and too much volume. This talk is what you build instead of pouring more into the same cup.

Do not split yourself in half

Opening the next location by being half-present in both places starves the first group and never gives the second one a culture. Find one leader. Hand them the tools. Support them. You stay with the work you already run. The organization gets bigger without a harder skeleton.

Add value, then ask if it arrived

The follow-up is a short call. Did you get the thing. It is not a cold pitch and it is not a script that asks someone to do a job you would hate if it were done to you. The people who respond are already in motion.
